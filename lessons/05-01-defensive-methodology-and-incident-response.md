# 05-01: Defensive Methodology and Incident Response

## Overview
Defensive security is a proactive and reactive discipline focused on protecting assets from unauthorized access and responding to security breaches. A cornerstone of incident response is the PICERL framework.

## The PICERL Cycle
The PICERL acronym represents the six phases of the Incident Response Life Cycle:

1.  **Preparation**: Establishing the tools, policies, and procedures required to handle an incident. This includes training, securing systems, and setting up monitoring capabilities.
2.  **Identification**: Detecting a potential security incident and determining its scope and severity. This involves analyzing logs, alerts, and system behavior.
3.  **Containment**: Limiting the damage caused by the incident. This can be short-term (isolating a system) or long-term (rebuilding or patching).
4.  **Eradication**: Removing the root cause of the incident, such as deleting malware, closing vulnerabilities, and removing unauthorized accounts.
5.  **Recovery**: Restoring affected systems to normal operation. This phase includes verifying that the systems are clean and functioning correctly.
6.  **Lessons Learned**: Post-incident analysis to improve future response efforts. Documenting what happened, how it was handled, and what can be done better.

## Importance of Structured Response
Following a structured methodology ensures that no critical steps are missed during the high-pressure environment of a security breach. It facilitates communication among stakeholders and provides a clear roadmap for recovery.
